# 14 — Seven experiments, two corpora, and which conclusions survived

Every table below is generated by `scripts/experiments/` and written to
`docs/experiments/`. Nothing here is hand-typed:

```bash
python -m scripts.experiments.run_all           # both corpora
python -m scripts.experiments.exp4_threshold    # or one at a time
```

Experiments 1, 2, 6 and 7 run against **both** corpora, because those are the
ones whose conclusions could plausibly be an artifact of an 85-chunk repo:

| corpus | files | function chunks | gold queries |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| OpsSense | 39 | 85 | 32 (human-reviewed) |
| httpx | 86 | 1465 | 20 (agent-written, **not yet reviewed**) |

The httpx gold set is `eval/httpx.json`. I wrote it from the parsed symbol list
and nobody has checked it, unlike OpsSense's. `tests/test_gold_set.py` proves
every label resolves to a chunk that exists, which catches typos and rot, not
bad judgement. Treat httpx as a replication check, not as an independent
result.

## What replicated

### Chunking strategy is the biggest single lever (Experiment 2)

| strategy | OpsSense ndcg@5 | OpsSense trunc | httpx ndcg@5 | httpx trunc |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| file | 0.12 | 36% | **0.00** | 88% |
| class | 0.49 | 18% | 0.21 | 19% |
| **function** | **0.56** | 18% | **0.48** | 17% |
| fixed | 0.12 | 45% | **0.00** | 95% |

Function-level wins on both, and the margin *widens* with corpus size. On httpx,
file and fixed chunking score a literal zero — 88% and 95% of their chunks
exceed the model's 256 wordpieces, so the vectors describe the first third of
each chunk and nothing after. The truncation column is not a caveat, it is the
mechanism.

Note `fixed` at 60 lines is worse than `file` on both corpora. Line counts are a
bad proxy for token counts, and a window that splits a function in half is worse
than no structure at all.

### Embeddings beat lexical retrieval, and baselines matter (Experiment 6)

| retriever | OpsSense ndcg@5 | httpx ndcg@5 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| random | 0.01 | 0.00 |
| grep | 0.14 | 0.12 |
| bm25 | 0.28 | 0.20 |
| **vector** | **0.56** | **0.48** |
| hybrid_rrf | 0.45 | 0.43 |

Vector retrieval is 4x grep on both. That is the number that justifies the
index, and it is why `random` and `grep` are in the harness permanently: if
embeddings ever stop beating substring matching, I want the table to say so.

RRF loses to plain vector on both corpora, for the reason in docs/11 — it
weights a weak arm equally with a strong one. Weighted fusion's best setting
moves between corpora (w=0.5 on OpsSense, w=0.7 on httpx), which is itself the
finding: the weight is a hyperparameter fitted to a corpus, while RRF's
tuning-free appeal comes with an assumption it does not state.

### Cross-encoder reranking hurts code (Experiment 7)

| corpus | vector ndcg@5 | reranked ndcg@5 | cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| OpsSense | **0.56** | 0.33 | 244ms |
| httpx | **0.48** | 0.40 | 184ms |

Worse on both, 100x slower, and the per-kind split shows why: the same model
*improves* prose queries (hit@1 0.50 → 0.67) while collapsing on code
(0.48 → 0.14). Full analysis in docs/12.

## What did not replicate

### The code-trained embedding model (Experiment 1)

| model | OpsSense hit@1 | OpsSense hit@5 | httpx hit@1 | httpx hit@5 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| MiniLM-L6 (384d) | 0.46 | 0.75 | 0.31 | 0.69 |
| **bge-small-en-v1.5** (384d) | 0.50 | **0.82** | 0.38 | **0.81** |
| st-codesearch-distilroberta (768d) | **0.54** | 0.79 | 0.19 | 0.50 |

On OpsSense, the code-trained model has the best hit@1 and looks like the
obvious upgrade. On httpx it is **the worst model in the table by a wide
margin** — 0.50 hit@5 against bge-small's 0.81.

Had I run only the small corpus, I would have shipped that model on the strength
of eight queries' worth of difference. This is the entire justification for the
second corpus, and it is worth more than any of the results that did replicate.

`bge-small-en-v1.5` wins on both and is the one real recommendation of this
milestone: +7 points of hit@5 over MiniLM on OpsSense, +12 on httpx, same 384
dimensions, same storage, no query-time cost.

## The other three (OpsSense only)

### Top-k is a cost curve, not a quality curve (Experiment 3)

| k | hit@k | recall@k | context tokens |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 0.46 | 0.35 | 145 |
| 3 | 0.64 | 0.50 | 490 |
| **5** | 0.75 | 0.65 | 838 |
| 10 | 0.79 | 0.73 | 1682 |
| 20 | 0.89 | 0.84 | 3398 |

Recall only ever rises with k, so the metric cannot choose k — the right-hand
column does. Going 5 → 20 buys 14 points of hit and quadruples what an LLM must
read. MRR is flat from k=3 (0.53 → 0.57), meaning the extra depth adds answers
far down the list, not better ones at the top. k=5 is the knee.

### There is no usable similarity threshold (Experiment 4)

| score | n | min | p25 | median | p75 | max |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| relevant | 32 | 0.281 | 0.338 | 0.416 | 0.477 | 0.684 |
| irrelevant | 128 | 0.103 | 0.248 | 0.358 | 0.430 | 0.583 |

The distributions overlap almost completely: the irrelevant p75 (0.430) sits
above the relevant median (0.416). Any cutoff that removes junk removes answers.

| threshold | answers kept | unanswerable correctly abstained |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0.25 | 0.75 | 0.50 |
| 0.30 | 0.71 | 0.75 |
| **0.35** | 0.64 | **1.00** |
| 0.40 | 0.50 | 1.00 |

Perfect abstention costs 11 points of hit@5. There is no setting where both
columns are good, which is why no library ships a default threshold and why
anyone who quotes one is quoting their corpus.

### Filters are worth exactly as much as they are correct (Experiment 5)

| variant | hit@5 | ndcg@5 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| no filter | 0.75 | 0.56 |
| blanket `language=python` | 0.61 | 0.47 |
| oracle (the gold answer's language) | **0.82** | **0.62** |

A perfect query router would gain 7 points of hit@5; a plausible-but-wrong
blanket filter loses 14. The oracle needs the answer to choose the filter, so it
is an upper bound, not a feature — and the gap between the two rows is the
budget available to anything that tries to guess the filter automatically.

## Caveats I am not going to bury

- **28 answerable queries on OpsSense, 16 on httpx.** One query is 3.6 and 6.3
  points respectively. Differences smaller than that are noise, and several
  gaps above are exactly that size. The replicated findings are the trustworthy
  ones, which is why this page is organised by what replicated.
- **The httpx gold set is unreviewed.** It agrees with OpsSense on the four
  experiments it ran, which is evidence it is not nonsense, not evidence it is
  right.
- **One embedding model family, one cross-encoder, one language.** Everything
  here is about Python and English queries.
